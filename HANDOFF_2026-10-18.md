## 1. Project direction and guardrails

This is a practical production ERP for a furniture and wood-products factory, not a generic SaaS demo. Replace the Excel-driven workflow with a working business process first. Keep the MVP practical and avoid getting stuck in obscure edge cases when they do not block the real workflow. User wants concrete next steps and runnable commands, no unnecessary repeated database inspections, and no emojis.

### Approved architecture

- Desktop client: Avalonia UI, C#/.NET, Arabic-first RTL; English support later.
- API/backend: NestJS with TypeScript, modular monolith.
- Central database: PostgreSQL; authoritative for official posted transactions.
- Local database: SQLite for local records/drafts and planned durable sync queue.
- Hosting direction: DigitalOcean + Docker.
- Critical posts must be validated centrally; transaction-related server effects should be atomic.

### Business rules to preserve

- Moving-average inventory valuation; prevent negative stock by default.
- Raw-material and finished-goods warehouses are separate.
- Raw materials/accessories tracked in inventory must not be conflated with industrial expenses.
- Industrial expenses and wages are allocated according to the approved plank-value allocation concept; excluded accessories do not automatically become administrative expenses.
- Both make-to-order and make-to-stock production are required.
- Completed accepted goods move to finished goods; unfinished production remains WIP at period close.
- Posted history should be corrected through traceable reversals/adjustments, not silently overwritten.
- Open accounting/tax/WIP/allocations questions remain open unless confirmed by the factory/accountant.

## 3. Real engineering workflow from the client

The engineer receives a picture/reference of the finished product, then produces:

1. A dimensioned SketchUp drawing of the furniture.
2. Several cut-list/nesting layout images. These show component dimensions and how pieces fit on a sheet; sample drawings show a board about 242 × 121 cm and MDF 17 mm, with material and quantity noted. These are examples only; always use the dimensions/units shown in the actual deliverable.
3. Material/board requirements including material/type, color/finish, thickness, board dimensions and board count.
4. BOM/accessory requirements such as hinges, drawer runners and other hardware. One exact phrase from the client about accessory details was ambiguous; do not hardcode an interpretation without a quick clarification when it affects the workflow.

The engineering tab must represent these as a technical package. The source image is technical evidence; structured fields are needed for later ERP use. Do not claim that the ERP automatically extracts measurements from images or re-runs the engineer's nesting optimization. The practical MVP is to upload the engineer's output and manually record/confirm its values.
